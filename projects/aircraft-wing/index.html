Finite Element Analysis of a Light Aircraft Wing

Executive Summary

  • Objective: Comprehensive structural integrity assessment and dynamic characterization of a composite light-aircraft wing under aerodynamic loads[cite: 4, 147].
  • Tools & Methodology: ANSYS Workbench integrating Fluent (CFD), ACP (Composite PrePost), Mechanical (Static, Modal, Harmonic), and Direct Optimization[cite: 41, 69, 147].
  • Key Achievements: Successfully mapped 3D CFD pressure fields onto a structural shell/beam model. Evaluated composite failure utilizing Tsai-Wu and Tsai-Hill criteria. Conducted parametric optimization of spar cross-sections and analyzed the pre-stress stiffening effect on natural frequencies[cite: 160, 170].

1. Computational Framework & Composite Setup

The structural model represents a light aircraft wing featuring a USA35B airfoil, a span of $10.82 \text{ m}$, and a cruise velocity of $48 \text{ m/s}$[cite: 33]. The internal architecture comprises multiple spars, ribs, and stringers modeled as beam elements (Aluminum Alloy), while the external skin is modeled using shell elements[cite: 121, 122, 191].

To accurately capture the anisotropic behavior of the wing skin, ANSYS ACP was employed to model an Epoxy Carbon UD Prepreg layup[cite: 123, 139]. The symmetric stacking sequence was defined to optimize torsional stiffness and bending resistance:

  • Layup Sequence: $[0 / +45 / -45 / +90 / +90 / -45 / +45 / 0] \times 2$ [cite: 139]
  • Ply Thickness: $0.125 \text{ mm}$ [cite: 139]

2. Fluid-Structure Interaction & Static Analysis

A one-way Fluid-Structure Interaction (FSI) approach was utilized. Aerodynamic loads were extracted from an ANSYS Fluent simulation conducted at an Angle of Attack (AoA) of $10^{\circ}$[cite: 147]. The resulting pressure field, generating $11832.5 \text{ N}$ of lift and $771.7 \text{ N}$ of drag, was directly mapped onto the mechanical solver as an imported pressure load[cite: 147, 149].

Structural integrity was evaluated using composite-specific failure criteria. The Inverse Reserve Factor (IRF) was calculated utilizing both Tsai-Wu and Tsai-Hill criteria. The initial design exhibited a peak IRF of $1.0019$, indicating an imminent risk of localized matrix/fiber failure under the simulated maximum load conditions.

3. Parametric Structural Optimization

To mitigate the critical IRF and minimize weight without compromising structural rigidity, a direct parametric optimization was performed on the rectangular cross-sections of the internal spars[cite: 69, 117, 162].

The optimization successfully identified a candidate configuration that reduced peak combined stresses while keeping structural deformation within operational tolerances.

4. Dynamic Characterization

4.1 Pre-Stressed Modal Analysis

The modal parameters of the wing were evaluated. Crucially, a pre-stressed modal analysis was conducted to account for the stiffness variations induced by the aerodynamic steady-state loading. The structural tensioning generated by the lift force resulted in a noticeable upward shift in the natural frequencies, commonly known as stress-stiffening[cite: 170, 184].

4.2 Harmonic Response Analysis

A full-method Harmonic Response analysis was subsequently performed over a frequency range of $10 \div 60 \text{ Hz}$ to evaluate the structural behavior under dynamic excitation[cite: 174]. A structural damping coefficient of $0.05$ was applied[cite: 181].

The Frequency Response Functions (FRFs) for equivalent Von Mises stresses were extracted, clearly identifying the resonant amplification peaks corresponding to the pre-stressed natural frequencies extracted in the prior step, providing critical data for aeroelastic stability and flutter prevention[cite: 180, 184].
